## What is ordered

The process and the operator's words are on `approval-record:loom-wave-2026-11-21-w8` ("How the
batches run"): five stories per batch, one review per batch on the merged diff, one integration
branch (`wave/2026-11-21-w7`), no publication of `main` between batches, one pull request and one
version at the end.

Session `runbook` (formerly `sdc`), orchestrator of session `loom` by the operator's word
(`approval-record:sdc-orchestrates-loom-2026-11-21`), 2026-11-21, in its triage of the 117 stories
that were not implemented, ordered eleven waves. Its wave 2 is this batch. Its decisions that
shape the batch, same day:

- "`story:tree-run-command` is built UNCUT, after record copy, the retry story and the case-id
  story. No cut version with refusals".
- Every story drafted for this run is accepted on drafting, as the ten proposed stories of
  `epic:protocol-tree-on-bindings` were.
- The story the triage calls G1 is split: the part in `crates/loom-bindings` is
  `story:bound-program-contract` (this batch); the part that needs the tree is
  `story:writer-not-called-stop` (the next batch).
- No variable `LOOM_STORE_DIR` for a bound program. The exit classes of a bound program are a
  list its binding states; Loom ships no default list.
- `story:protocol-argument-schema`: Loom writes the check of a value against a declared argument
  schema, because Canon exports none; the story says it moves to Canon if Canon exports one.
- `story:release-binaries`: the workflow builds with `contents: read` and keeps the binaries and
  their checksums as workflow artifacts. Which identity attaches them to a release is the
  operator's decision and is not part of this batch; the story is built without an upload.
